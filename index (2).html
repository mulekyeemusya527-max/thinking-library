Aristotle · 4th century BCE

Aristotle's inquiry into what the best human life is, treating happiness as an activity of the soul in accordance with virtue and examining the virtues of character and thought. This edition is W. D. Ross's English translation.

On the Shortness of Life

Seneca · c. 49 CE (date uncertain)

An essay addressed to a friend, Paulinus, arguing that life is long enough if used well and that people waste time on trivial pursuits. It is one of Seneca's moral essays, known as the Dialogues.

Free to read in your browser at Standard Ebooks, inside Seneca's Dialogues in Aubrey Stewart's 1889 translation. Believed public domain in the United States; readers elsewhere should check local law.

Viktor E. Frankl · 1946 (English translation 1959)

A memoir of the author's experiences as a prisoner in Nazi concentration camps, followed by an introduction to logotherapy, his approach to psychotherapy centred on the search for meaning.

About and editorial policy

The Thinking Library exists to make knowledge accessible and to encourage independent thought. It informs readers and does not tell them what to believe.

How content is labelled

  • Established facts: supported by the evidence cited.
  • Interpretations: explanations that reasonable people can contest.
  • Disputed claims: claims on which sources or experts disagree.
  • Open questions: what is not yet known.

News reports events. Essays, research notes and commentary are kept in a separate section.

Principles

  • Competing arguments are presented fairly, without false balance where evidence is strong.
  • Headlines are factual, not sensational.
  • Uncertainty and corrections are stated openly.
  • Sources are named and linked; unverifiable claims are excluded or labelled.

Copyright

The library links to public-domain texts, open editions and authorised sources. It does not reproduce copyrighted books or other publishers' articles without permission. News items are original summaries that link to the source.
